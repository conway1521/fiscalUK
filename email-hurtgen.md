Dear Dr Hürtgen,

I am a PhD candidate working on the timing of UK tax policy, and I am writing to ask whether
the measure-level dataset behind Cloyne, Dimsdale, Postel-Vinay and Hürtgen (2024), "Tax Changes
in the United Kingdom 2009-2020: A New Narrative Account and Dataset", is available to
researchers.

I have been working with the public narrative record: Cloyne's 1945-2009 dataset, the interwar
measure-level file in the Zenodo replication package for the 2023 Review of Economic Studies
paper, and the quarterly series and macro data in the Harvard Dataverse deposit for the 2025
JPE paper. My own contribution is a measure-level coding of UK tax measures from Budget 2004 to
Autumn 2018, built on the same Major and Minor motive scheme, which I use to study the interval
between announcement and implementation rather than the size of the measures.

The first paper from that work documents a change in the anticipation content of UK fiscal
policy. The revenue-weighted share of the tax impulse announced at least 120 days before taking
effect rises from 0.18 in 1945-79 to 0.70 since 2000, and the unanticipated component of the
quarterly impulse falls by roughly a factor of five. I should say that I found the same pattern
in your own aggregated series before I found it in mine: the ratio of your baseline and
unanticipated exogenous series gives 0.25, 0.42 and 0.70 across the same eras, and 0.02 for
1920-44. The paper credits the anticipated and unanticipated distinction to Cloyne (2013)
following Mertens and Ravn (2012), and treats the replication as external validation rather
than as my own result.

The reason for writing is that the published Dataverse package ships aggregated quarterly
series, so it carries implementation dates but not announcement dates. For the second paper I
need announcement-dated measures, and 2009 to 2020 is the one window where no public
measure-level source exists. If the dataset behind the 2024 working paper could be shared, it
would let me do two things I cannot otherwise do: extend the announcement-dated series past
2018, and cross-validate my own 2004-2018 coding against an independent one over the 2009-2018
overlap. I would of course cite the dataset paper as the source and would be glad to share
whatever the comparison turns up, including any disagreements between the two codings, which
may be of some use to you as well.

If the data is not available for release at this stage, I quite understand, and I would be
grateful simply to know that, so that I can plan around it.

With thanks for making the rest of the record public. It has been genuinely useful.
