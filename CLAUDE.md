# UK tax timing

Working repository for two research papers by Alessandro Conway: The
Anticipation Content of Fiscal Policy (AC-WP-2021-01), in
paper/fiscaluk-paper1.tex, and When to Raise Taxes (AC-WP-2026-06), in
paper/fiscaluk-paper2.tex. The research plan is in PLAN.md.

## Writing rules

Hard requirements, not preferences. The aim is prose that reads as mine,
made clearer and more precise than a first draft, and written to be read.

**Sentences and paragraphs**

- Vary sentence length. Most sentences sit between fifteen and thirty
  words, a long sentence is used where the reasoning needs it, and none
  runs past about fifty words. A short sentence may open or move an
  argument, and is never used as a punchline.
- No fragments, no drumbeats of three or four words, no aphorisms, and no
  sentence built to be quoted.
- A paragraph usually ends on its next substantive point. It may
  occasionally end on a summary, which helps the variety.
- Keep a thread of narrative: why the question arose, what the evidence
  made possible, and what turned up along the way. The narrative moves
  differ from one paper to the next, so that none of them becomes a
  template.

**Constructions to avoid**

- No em dashes anywhere. Use a comma, a colon, a full stop, or
  parentheses.
- "Rather than" is fine as a plain comparison. Neither it, nor "X, not Y",
  nor "X and not Y" is used as a corrective landing.
- No clefts ("What X is, is Y"), no counted announcements ("Three findings
  follow"), and no series of clauses built on the same opening word.
- No abstractions that speak: an account, a record, a framework, a survey,
  or a test does not say, ask, know, or see.
- No metaphors for economic objects: no channels, pipelines, or levers,
  and no flows or measures that carry, reach, arrive, weigh, or feed. A
  vivid word about people, events, or the literature is welcome where it
  is the kind of word I would use.
- No stock phrases of generated prose, among them "it turns out",
  "genuinely", "crucially", "notably", and "sheds light".

**Register**

- Hedging is British understatement: "roughly", "appears", "on balance",
  "a fair idea". Defensive disclaimers that a result might be wrong are
  cut, and a limit is stated once, plainly, where it applies.
- First person follows the paper. Some papers say "I" and some do not,
  and that difference is kept.
- Oxford comma throughout.
- Headings are plain nouns ("Data", "State dependence", "Limits"), never
  sentences.
- Code, scripts, engines, and file paths belong in the README. They
  appear in a footnote only where a reader cannot follow the paper
  without them, and never in the body.
- Never the word "honest" or any form of it.
- No self-congratulation, especially about publishing openly.
- Never claim a status, component, or result that does not exist.
